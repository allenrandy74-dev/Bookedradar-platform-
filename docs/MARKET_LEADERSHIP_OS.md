# BookedRadar Market Leadership Operating System

Updated: 2026-09-27

## Category ambition
BookedRadar is not positioned as a commodity AI receptionist.

Category definition:
**Home-Service Opportunity Platform**

Core promise:
**Catch the opportunity. Route the right ones to people. Recover the ones that go quiet. Prove what happened.**

The market-leadership goal is directional, not a claim. Do not publicly call BookedRadar the market leader until independent market evidence supports that statement.

## Product pillars
1. **Answer** — reliable after-hours/overflow intake.
2. **Escalate** — immediate human escape and safety/high-value routing.
3. **Recover** — stale estimates, missed opportunities, cancellations and approved follow-up.
4. **Prove** — RadarProof separates captured, engaged, booked, recovered and revenue-confirmed outcomes.
5. **Deploy** — trade-specific, low-friction onboarding and controlled acceptance testing.
6. **Learn** — every customer and demo generates structured evidence for improving workflows without weakening safety/privacy controls.

## North-star business metric
**Confirmed customer value created per active customer**, backed by auditable source evidence.

Do not use AI call count as the north star.

Supporting outcome metrics:
- qualified opportunities captured
- human escalations completed
- estimates re-engaged
- appointments/acceptances confirmed
- revenue confirmed by a trusted source
- customer hours/interruption reduced where measurable

## Funnel metrics
Track by prospect and trade:
- qualified prospect

- paid conversion

- expansion to additional BookedRadar capability

Primary acquisition metric:
**positive conversations per 20 qualified prospects**

Secondary:
**Proof Pilots per 20 qualified prospects**

## Pilot scorecard
Before pilot:
- baseline after-hours/overflow workflow documented
- expected call volume
- escalation rules approved
- services/service area approved
- existing system of record identified
- acceptance test passed

During pilot:
- calls offered
- calls answered
- qualified opportunities captured
- urgent/human transfers
- incomplete calls requiring follow-up
- estimates/opportunities re-engaged
- errors/false classifications
- customer feedback

End of pilot:
- measurable value created
- operational friction
- trust/quality feedback
- recommended configuration changes
- continue / expand / stop decision by customer

Never convert a pilot automatically to paid service unless the commercial agreement explicitly authorizes it.

## Reliability targets
Leadership requires measurable operational quality.

Internal targets to work toward:
- production automated test pass: 100% before deployment
- tenant readiness: zero blockers before live traffic
- no secret/PII leakage in logs
- human transfer path continuously acceptance-tested
- no invented appointment availability
- no invented pricing
- no automated claim of recovered revenue without evidence
- graceful failure path for CRM/email/SMS dependencies
- documented restore/rollback procedure
- acceptance test for every new tenant

Targets are internal engineering goals, not customer SLAs until contractually defined.

## Research / experiment cadence
Every two weeks:
1. Review competitor product/pricing/positioning changes.
2. Review prospect objections.
3. Review demo-to-pilot conversion.
4. Review pilot quality and customer feedback.
5. Choose no more than 1–2 meaningful experiments.
6. Define success metric before implementation.
7. Preserve a control/cohort when practical.
8. Ship only after automated + acceptance tests.
9. Record result: keep / revise / revert.

## Proof Pilot hypothesis
For qualified Founding Partner prospects:
- after-hours/overflow scope first
- up to 14 days or 25 real calls, whichever comes first
- no setup fee during the proof period
- daytime workflow remains untouched unless prospect requests otherwise
- human escalation preserved
- confirm-only scheduling unless live scheduling has separately passed customer acceptance
- pilot scorecard reviewed before paid continuation

Do not publish as an unconditional offer until customer agreement, usage limits, abuse prevention and commercial wording are approved.

## Competitive moat roadmap
### Moat 1 — RadarProof
Make attribution trustworthy enough that owners can see why BookedRadar is worth paying for.

### Moat 2 — Recovery intelligence
Go beyond answering into estimate/lead lifecycle recovery.

### Moat 3 — Trade playbooks
HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, multi-trade and later adjacent verticals should have proven intake, safety, escalation and recovery patterns.

### Moat 4 — Plug-and-play deployment
Turn onboarding questionnaire -> validated tenant config -> acceptance tests -> go-live checklist into a repeatable deployment system.

### Moat 5 — Human + AI operating model
Make human escalation a strength rather than treating it as AI failure.

### Moat 6 — Integration depth
Connect to the systems contractors already use rather than forcing BookedRadar to become their entire field-service system.

## Feature decision rule
A feature earns priority when it materially improves one of:
- customer acquisition
- trust
- intake quality
- response speed
- human handoff
- recovery
- proof/attribution
- onboarding speed
- retention/expansion
- reliability/security/compliance

Avoid feature work whose primary benefit is "competitor X has it" unless customers treat it as table stakes.

## Public positioning guardrails
Do:
- show live demos
- state what the platform actually does
- use evidence-backed proof
- explain human escalation
- show trade-specific workflows
- use customer results only with permission and source evidence

Do not:
- claim market leadership before independently supportable
- guarantee revenue
- say "never miss a lead"
- imply AI replaces dispatchers/CSRs
- fabricate testimonials
- present synthetic demo recovery as customer revenue
