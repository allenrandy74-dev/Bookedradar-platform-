# BookedRadar Customer Growth & Retention Playbook

Updated: 2026-09-27

## Principle
Growth is a closed loop:
**Acquire -> Activate -> Prove -> Retain -> Expand -> Refer -> Acquire**

No stage is allowed to operate without a measurable next outcome.

## 1. Acquire
Target ICP accounts with a documented workflow signal, not bulk lists.
For each prospect record:
- trade
- geography
- decision maker/contact route
- after-hours/overflow signal
- high-ticket estimate/financing signal
- current software/answering signal when public
- one BookedRadar hypothesis
- outreach variant
- response/objection

First-touch rule:
- personalized
- value/demo first
- one CTA
- ask for interest, not a calendar commitment
- do not make unsupported "you are losing calls/revenue" claims

## 2. Activate
Customer activation milestone:
**First successfully handled real opportunity with a clean customer-approved handoff and auditable proof.**

Onboarding is not complete merely because configuration fields are filled.

Track:
- signed/approved scope
- questionnaire complete
- tenant generated
- customer rules approved
- acceptance tests passed
- forwarding/integration enabled
- first real opportunity handled
- first-value timestamp

Goal: minimize time from commercial approval to first value without skipping acceptance tests.

## 3. Prove
At pilot and ongoing reviews, show:
- calls/opportunities handled
- qualified opportunities captured
- human escalations
- estimates re-engaged
- bookings/acceptances when confirmed
- confirmed revenue only from trusted evidence
- failures and incomplete outcomes, not only successes

Never cherry-pick only successful interactions.

## 4. Retain
Customer health signals:
GREEN
- system healthy
- customer seeing measurable use/value
- no unresolved high-severity issues
- expected usage
- customer engages with reviews/check-ins

YELLOW
- usage materially below expected
- repeated incomplete handoffs
- unresolved integration issue
- no first value after onboarding
- customer stops engaging
- repeated billing failure

RED
- explicit dissatisfaction/cancellation intent
- serious service failure
- recurring incorrect routing
- trust/privacy complaint
- unresolved billing dispute
- production outage materially affecting customer

Response:
- RED: same-business-day owner review; no automated upsell/review request
- YELLOW: investigate root cause and agree corrective action
- GREEN: continue value review; consider expansion/referral only after demonstrated value

## 5. Customer cadence
New customer:
- acceptance test before live traffic
- first-value review after first meaningful real opportunity
- day 7: quality/friction check
- day 14: proof review for proof-pilot customers
- day 30: outcome review
- monthly thereafter for early-stage customers

Do not turn these into empty "checking in" messages. Every touch should contain evidence, a question, a fix, or a useful recommendation.

## 6. Expand
Only recommend expansion when evidence supports it.
Examples:
- after-hours -> overflow when after-hours quality is proven
- Answer -> Recover when open estimates/missed opportunities exist
- Recover -> scheduling when capacity rules/integrations are ready
- one location -> additional locations after stable operation

Expansion metric:
additional confirmed customer value, not feature adoption for its own sake.

## 7. Reviews, testimonials and referrals
Ask only after a clear positive value event or positive review conversation.

Sequence:
1. Confirm customer is satisfied.
2. Ask permission for a review/testimonial.
3. Make the request easy.
4. Ask for referral only when the customer has experienced enough value to recommend BookedRadar confidently.
5. Never incentivize a review in a way that requires positive sentiment.
6. Never fabricate or rewrite a customer's words as a testimonial without approval.

## 8. Win/loss/churn learning
For every meaningful loss or cancellation, classify primary reason:
- no problem / low volume
- trust
- call quality
- human escalation
- integration
- onboarding complexity
- existing vendor
- price
- timing
- feature gap
- reliability
- compliance/legal
- other

Record verbatim customer explanation where permission/context allows.
Do not immediately build the requested feature. Look for repeated patterns.

## 9. Reliability is retention
Customer-success process must surface:
- failed actions
- CRM/email/SMS dependency failures
- transfer failures
- tenant readiness blockers
- unexpected usage drops
- unresolved support messages
- billing state problems

A customer should hear about a material BookedRadar problem from us before discovering it repeatedly themselves whenever our monitoring can detect it.

## 10. Weekly operating review
Review:
- new qualified prospects
- positive conversations
- demo calls
- proof pilots
- activations / time-to-first-value
- customer health
- confirmed value created
- support/reliability incidents
- churn/at-risk reasons
- referrals/reviews
- experiment results

Pick the smallest set of actions that attacks the current bottleneck.

## 11. Monthly leadership review
Ask:
- What is the acquisition bottleneck?
- What is the activation bottleneck?
- Why did customers stay?
- Why did customers hesitate or leave?
- Which trade is producing the best value?
- Which workflow produces the strongest proof?
- What is becoming table stakes?
- What is becoming our moat?
- What should we stop doing?

## Guardrail
"Proven business practice" means evidence-backed and appropriate to BookedRadar's stage. It does not mean blindly copying tactics, spamming prospects, dark patterns, fake scarcity, fake reviews, hidden renewals, or building every competitor feature.
