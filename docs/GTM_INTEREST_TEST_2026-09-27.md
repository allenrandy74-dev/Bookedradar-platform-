# BookedRadar Customer Interest Research & GTM Test — 2026-09-27

## Research conclusion
Do not position BookedRadar primarily as "an AI receptionist." The category is increasingly commoditized and price-anchored by low-cost/free answering products and field-service platforms with embedded AI.

Lead with the operational outcome:
1. Catch after-hours and overflow demand while the customer still needs help.
2. Give callers a fast path to a human for urgent/high-value/ambiguous situations.
3. Recover estimates and leads that go quiet.
4. Prove what was captured, engaged, booked, recovered and actually confirmed as revenue.

## Evidence used
- Invoca 2026 home-services benchmark: only 52% of inbound home-service calls reach a person overall; HVAC answer rate in its dataset is 34%, plumbing 74%. 38% of answered calls are leads and 45% of those leads convert on the call.
- ServiceTitan: top-performing contractors book 62% of inbound lead calls vs 39% for others.
- Housecall Pro homeowner research: response time matters to 97% of surveyed homeowners; 53% are comfortable with AI handling the initial call/chat.
- ServiceTitan 2026 AI research: 12% have embedded AI operationally; 34% are experimenting; trust/complexity remain barriers.
- Current competitors increasingly offer free/low-cost AI answering. This makes answering alone a weak differentiation.
- Gong cold-outreach research: first-touch messages should offer value / ask for interest rather than lead with a meeting request.

## Recommended ICP for first customers
Prioritize independent, locally owned HVAC/plumbing/electrical/roofing businesses that:
- have enough demand to feel call/follow-up leakage;
- are not already deeply locked into an enterprise AI/contact-center stack;
- advertise emergency, after-hours or same-day service;
- have weekday office hours or obvious peak/surge periods;
- offer financing, estimates, memberships or high-ticket replacement work;
- have roughly 50–250 public reviews as a useful prospecting signal, not a hard qualification;
- have an identifiable owner/GM/operations manager.

Deprioritize for initial pilots:
- large multi-location groups already running sophisticated ServiceTitan/Podium/Avoca call-center systems;
- tiny operators with very low call volume and little estimate pipeline;
- franchises where local management cannot approve the technology.

## New offer structure to test
### First touch
Do not ask for 20 minutes.
Give the prospect a trade-specific live demo they can try immediately.

Example:
"Saw that you handle generator and electrical work across Southeast Texas. I built a live electrical demo for the exact after-hours problem we solve. Call (409) 213-9980 and say: 'Half my house lost power and I smell something hot near the panel.' It will capture the situation and show the human-escalation path. Worth sending you the 2-minute overview?"

Target first-touch length: 60–100 words.
One CTA only: interest / permission to send more.

### Second touch
Send the Revenue Leak Audit or a 90-second personalized screen/video walkthrough tied to one public workflow signal from that business.

### Third touch
Offer a 15-minute workflow review only after engagement.

### Pilot
Test a qualified, capped proof pilot rather than immediately asking an unknown business to trust BookedRadar with all calls.
Recommended hypothesis:
- after-hours/overflow only;
- 14 days or 25 real calls, whichever comes first;
- no setup fee;
- existing daytime workflow untouched;
- human transfer preserved;
- confirm-only scheduling until separately accepted;
- simple before/after RadarProof report;
- customer can stop after proof period;
- paid continuation uses the selected package.

Final legal/commercial wording must be reviewed before publishing.

## Product changes to consider
### Do now / low risk
- Add optional transparent virtual-assistant identification in demo greetings and test customer reaction.
- Keep immediate "talk to a person" transfer.
- Keep confirm-only appointment requests as the default until real scheduling acceptance is complete.
- Build a prospect-facing proof report that compares baseline missed/after-hours opportunities with pilot results.
- Add per-tenant "overflow only / after-hours only / all calls" routing mode to onboarding if not already explicit.

### Test before changing
- AI disclosure wording: compare natural "virtual assistant" identification against current greeting in demos.
- 14-day/25-call qualified proof pilot.
- Short email + live-demo CTA vs current long email + meeting CTA.
- Owner/GM direct outreach vs general inbox.
- Local relationship channels: trade associations, supply houses, chambers, and referrals.

### Do not change yet
- Do not lower the $149 RadarAnswer price solely to match commodity tools.
- Do not reduce RadarRecover pricing until pilot data shows price resistance rather than trust friction.
- Do not make autonomous scheduling the lead promise.
- Do not claim guaranteed revenue or "never miss a lead."
- Do not replace human escalation with more automation.

## 10-prospect A/B experiment
Next 10 qualified prospects:
- 5 receive 60–100 word personalized email with a live trade-demo number and interest CTA.
- 5 receive the same short structure plus a personalized Revenue Leak Audit observation.
Track:
- delivered / bounced
- reply
- positive reply
- demo call observed
- audit opened/completed where measurable
- meeting accepted
- pilot accepted
- stated objection (trust, existing vendor, price, integration, no need, timing)

Do not optimize on open rate alone. Primary metric: positive conversations per qualified prospect.

## Decision rules
After 20–30 qualified prospects:
- Low replies + no demo calls -> message/channel problem.
- Replies but no demos -> demo CTA/friction problem.
- Demos but no pilots -> trust/offer/risk-reversal problem.
- Pilots but no paid conversion -> product/value/pricing problem.
- Paid conversions but onboarding pain -> plug-and-play/integration problem.

This prevents changing price or product before identifying the actual bottleneck.
